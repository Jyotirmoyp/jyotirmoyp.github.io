Workshop: Understanding Earth's Deep Interior

Goal: Earth is the only rocky planet in the solar system that exhibits plate tectonics: its outer shell, the lithosphere, is broken into plates that move relative to one another. What drives that motion has long been debated. Modern seismological networks across the globe now let us image Earth's interior, and from these images we can interpret its density structure — showing how regions of downwelling and upwelling are distributed, potentially pulling and pushing the plates at the surface.

In this workshop, we investigate how to numerically calculate surface motion from density anomalies obtained from seismic measurements, integrating Earth's interior dynamics with GPS measurements.

Computational Geodynamics

Syllabus: Introduction to scientific modelling; governing principles of mantle convection models; geophysical quantities; solving differential equations using the finite-difference method; solution of the heat conduction equation in 2D; basic plotting techniques in GMT; plate reconstruction using GPlates.

Course level: M.Tech, as part of a Geophysics course.
Centre for Earth Sciences, IISc Bangalore.
